\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
This work decomposes SQLi WAF bypass analysis along semantics, scale, and structure under an empirical, live-environment search distribution.

\subsection{Research Question Answers}
\textbf{RQ1 (Scale and semantics):} Our comprehensive live execution across $N=22{,}230$ payload variants revealed an aggregate WAF perimeter evasion rate of $77.9\%$ ($17{,}309/22{,}230$), decomposing into a $47.7\%$ clean exploit bypass rate (HTTP 200 data leakage) and a $30.2\%$ database-layer error reach rate (HTTP 500), with zero 400/422 validation rejections ($N_{400/422}=0$). Crucially, adaptive mutation yielded an evolutionary gain of $+40.7\%$ in clean exploitation over unmutated Generation 0 baseline seeds (established without survivorship bias), while an independent structural ground-truth audit confirmed $100.0\%$ labeling precision across 400 sampled outcomes.

\textbf{RQ2 (Structure):} Evasion efficacy is intensely polarized across mutation categories. The \texttt{double\_url\_encode} ($N_f=7{,}683$) and \texttt{null\_byte} ($N_f=7{,}837$) families each achieved a $99.9\%$ edge bypass rate under the fixed AWS WAF managed ruleset. In contrast, structural space modifications (\texttt{tab\_newline}: $81.2\%$ blocked) and comment-block injections (\texttt{comment\_inline}: $84.6\%$ blocked; \texttt{comment\_slash}: $92.9\%$ blocked) were systematically suppressed. Furthermore, the \texttt{case\_mix} family ($86.4\%$ 404 responses) demonstrates that edge-evasion does not guarantee application exploitation. This proves that AWS WAF's lexical tokenization is well-optimized for syntactic delimiter anomalies, but exhibits fundamental vulnerabilities to encoding-depth discrepancies.

\textbf{RQ3 (Defense and Normalization):} Offline canonicalization controls demonstrate that naive single- or multi-layer RFC percent-decoding fails to eliminate evasion risks, with a surrogate TF-IDF logistic-regression model achieving only 3 out of 7 correct predictions ($42.9\%$ accuracy) on test instances across both raw and canonicalized inputs. Effective defense necessitates semantic AST-level validation rather than superficial regex-based edge filtering.

\subsection{Implications and Future Work}
For defenders, WAF policy tuning and regression testing must be family-aware; relying on coarse aggregate dashboards obscures massive vulnerabilities in specific encoding vectors. Edge filters must be coupled with strict backend parameter binding and context-aware deserialization. Furthermore, our incidental discovery during exploratory testing of upstream 502 DoS errors induced by raw null-bytes demonstrates that adversarial mutation fuzzing can uncover availability vulnerabilities alongside confidentiality exploits. Future work should systematically evaluate cross-layer DoS resilience in managed cloud WAF architectures and extend multi-session variance measurement across geographically distributed deployments.

We presented a reproducible black-box methodology and empirical live-testbed analysis for managed WAF assessment. The core takeaway is that meaningful offensive measurement demands decomposed statistics and transparent search bias. Our family-level analysis identifies concrete operational blind spots, reinforcing the necessity of defense-in-depth security architectures.

\textbf{Limitations and Ethics.} All reported figures represent single-session point estimates bounded by our AWS WAF policy configuration and URL injection channel. All methodology was executed exclusively against authorized research infrastructure.
