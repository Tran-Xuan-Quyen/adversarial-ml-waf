\begin{abstract}
Attackers routinely evade Web Application Firewalls (WAFs) by mutating SQL injection (SQLi) payloads. Prior studies report high bypass rates, yet these aggregate figures often conflate heterogeneous operators and depend on search biases. Consequently, defenders need reproducible, family-resolved measurements tied to explicit labels.

This paper presents an offensive methodology that generates discrete SQLi variants through a dialect seed, deployment-specific augmentations, and learned transform rules. We issued black-box HTTP probes against a live AWS WAF-fronted application, collecting per-attempt JSONL logs. In our 22,230-variant deployment experiment, our operational classifier identified a 47.7\% clean bypass rate (HTTP 200 OK), with an additional 30.2\% of attempts inducing server-side errors (HTTP 500) that confirm database-layer reach, yielding a total WAF evasion rate of 77.9\%. Family-level analysis reveals extreme heterogeneity: double URL-encoding and null-byte cohorts achieved bypass rates exceeding 99.9\%, while space-replacement, tab/newline, and comment-block mutations were systematically suppressed (81.2\%--92.9\% blocked). An independent ground-truth audit across sampled responses verified 100.0\% classification precision.

As a secondary control, we evaluated repeated RFC percent-decoding against a TF-IDF logistic-regression surrogate on a 20-sample pilot (13 train / 7 test). The surrogate achieved an accuracy of 3/7 (42.9\%) on both raw and canonicalized inputs, suggesting on this pilot scale that naive decoding does not immediately rescue simple linear surrogate models. All reported rates represent single-session point estimates for this concrete deployment instance; the core contribution lies in the explicit methodology, decomposed statistics, and reproducible artifact.
\end{abstract}
